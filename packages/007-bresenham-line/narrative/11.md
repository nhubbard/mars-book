# Compare work without inventing a timing model

The incremental method avoids multiplication and division inside its pixel loop.
The alternative recomputes a rounded minor coordinate from a major-axis step number.
That arithmetic gives a useful independent way to derive the expected image.

For the default fixture, the assembled reference completes 145 simulated instructions
and the rational alternative 138, including setup and normal exit. The comparison
counts actual expanded machine instructions; source lines are not a substitute.
The alternative's lower count in this one fixture does not establish a shorter host
time, a lower hardware cycle count, or a win on every line.

MARS instruction counts assign one completed instruction to a multiply or divide just
as they do to an addition. They do not model the latency of a particular physical CPU.
This chapter therefore reports the measured workload and metric alongside the result.
An optimization claim needs that scope as well as identical output and preservation checks.
