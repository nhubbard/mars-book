# Bound the gap between seeing a target and stopping

Equality polling is safe here because the loop samples frequently enough for the
selected device rates. Its repeated path has three machine instructions: a coordinate
load, a conditional branch, and a `nop`. Once the load sees the target, the fall-through
branch and slot lead directly to the movement-stop store.

Consider the less favorable phase: the robot reaches the target in the completion
callback of a load that returned the previous coordinate. That comparison uses a
stale value, so it takes one more pass. The next load sees the target, and the stop
store occurs six instructions after the tick that reached it. With at least eight
instructions per tick, the stop happens before another pixel step.

A long calculation inserted after the read can break this bound. The robot may step
past the target before the stop command, or a slower polling loop may never sample
the exact coordinate. Feedback does not remove the need to reason about sampling
rate. The stated rate range and short loop are part of this example's stopping argument.
