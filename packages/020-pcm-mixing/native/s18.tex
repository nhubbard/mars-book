\section{Step 18 Finish the little-endian output sample}\label{pcm-s18}

Shift the result logically right by eight into \texttt{t1}, then store its low byte at output offset one. For a negative result, the shifted register has other set bits; \texttt{sb} selects precisely bits fifteen through eight of the original halfword. The output is now complete in the requested byte order, but the pointers and remaining count still describe this sample.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\subsection{Write and inspect}
Replace the temporary branch and nop in \texttt{store\_high}. Keep the surrounding labels and region markers.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\MARSCodeLabel{MIPS assembly}
\begin{minted}[samepage=true]{mars-idea}
    srl $t1, $t0, 8
    sb $t1, 1($a2)

Use recipe \texttt{s18-1}, \texttt{s18-2}, \texttt{s18-3} from the companion construction inputs. Pause at \texttt{mix\_ready} before comparing this partial state.

\subsection{Expected observation}
Positive saturation stores ff 7f. Negative saturation stores 00 80. Both have one clipping event.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\subsection{Check your understanding}
Why is the logical shift sufficient for a negative result?

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A} It converts saturation into wrapping.
  \item \textbf{B} It removes the need to count clips.
  \item \textbf{C} Only the selected next byte is stored.
\end{itemize}
