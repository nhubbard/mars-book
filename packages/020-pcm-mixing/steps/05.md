# Read the two bytes of input A

Load offsets zero and one from a0 with lbu. Each load yields an unsigned byte. Two separate byte operations permit a sample to begin at an odd address and make the logical two-byte boundary explicit. Do not interpret the high byte as a signed sample by itself: its sign bit belongs to the complete halfword.

## Write and inspect

Replace the temporary branch and nop in `load_a`. Keep surrounding labels and markers.

```asm
    lbu $t0, 0($a0)
    lbu $t1, 1($a0)
```

Use the `s05-*` input recipes from [the observation guide](../fixtures/guided-inputs.md). Begin a fresh Debug execution and pause at mix_ready.

## Expected observation

For A=-1000, the bytes are hexadecimal 18 fc: t0=24 and t1=252.

## Check your understanding

Why do the two bytes not each represent a separate sample?

- **A** Every byte is a complete sixteen-bit value.
- **B** Together they encode one signed halfword.
- **C** The loads advance the pointer automatically.
