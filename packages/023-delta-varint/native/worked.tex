\section{Follow the complete default byte stream}
The five default readings occupy twenty bytes as fixed-width words. Their deltas,
mapped values and complete byte sequences are:
\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{The default sequence encodes to eight bytes. All byte spellings are hexadecimal.}
  \tagpdfsetup{table/header-rows={1}}
  \begin{tabular}{rrrrl}
    \toprule
    Index & Reading & Delta  & Mapped value & Bytes             \\
    \midrule
    0     & 1000    & 1000   & 2000         & \texttt{d0 0f}    \\
    1     & 1002    & 2      & 4            & \texttt{04}       \\
    2     & 1001    & \(-1\) & 1            & \texttt{01}       \\
    3     & 1001    & 0      & 0            & \texttt{00}       \\
    4     & 20000   & 18999  & 37998        & \texttt{ee a8 02} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Concatenation gives \texttt{d0 0f 04 01 00 ee a8 02}. Byte positions zero and one
belong to the first reading; positions two, three and four each belong to one further
reading. The last reading occupies positions five through seven. The externally
supplied byte length is eight. There are no separators between values and no embedded
reading count in this codec.

To decode the first value, take payload 80 from \texttt{d0} and payload 15 from
\texttt{0f}, giving \(80+15\cdot128=2000\). Its low bit is zero, so the delta is
1000. Adding the initial predecessor zero reconstructs 1000. The next byte maps to
delta two and reconstructs 1002. The odd mapped value one reconstructs delta minus one.
Update the predecessor after each complete accepted reading.

\begin{MARSExercise}{Find the unit mismatch}
  A decoder returns four consumed input bytes and three produced readings after reaching
  its output capacity. Mark that prefix in the default stream and list its decoded
  readings. Explain why returning three as the consumed-byte count would resume inside
  a different logical position.
\end{MARSExercise}
