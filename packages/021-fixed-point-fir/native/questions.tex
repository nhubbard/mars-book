\section{Check the arithmetic before optimizing}
\begin{MARSExercise}{Two products that do not fit a word}
Compute the sum of two products whose sample and coefficient codes are both -32768.
Explain why copying a signed word dot-product result into a doubleword register after
the operation cannot recover the exact positive sum.
\end{MARSExercise}

\begin{MARSExercise}{A halfway negative result}
Compare accumulators -16384 and -16385. Apply the bias and floor division explicitly.
Then explain why truncation toward zero after adding the bias is wrong for one case.
\end{MARSExercise}

\begin{MARSExercise}{An asymmetric impulse}
Use the unequal coefficients from the impulse example. Identify the coefficient that
multiplies the impulse in outputs zero, one and seven. State which input-array index
contains the first new sample.
\end{MARSExercise}

\begin{MARSExercise}{A one-output vector call}
A caller requests one output and supplies seven history samples plus one current
sample. Determine whether a sixteen-byte input load fits its logical slice. Explain
why an eight-output eligibility test would be unnecessary for this vectorization.
\end{MARSExercise}

\begin{MARSExercise}{A split with no reset artifact}
Split a sixty-four-output filter call after output twenty-three. Give the two output
counts and the second input's starting byte offset relative to the original base.
Account for all seven history samples before its first new sample.
\end{MARSExercise}
