\section{Account for every sample window}
For an Up row, both cursors consume the same number of sample bytes. The selector
is outside this interval. The following extents describe a single RGB8 row:
\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{longtable}{p{20mm}p{30mm}p{30mm}p{30mm}p{30mm}}
\toprule Width & Sample bytes & Full groups of 16 & Tail bytes & Full row bytes \\\midrule
1 & 3 & 0 & 3 & 4 \\
5 & 15 & 0 & 15 & 16 \\
6 & 18 & 1 & 2 & 19 \\
17 & 51 & 3 & 3 & 52 \\
512 & 1536 & 96 & 0 & 1537 \\\bottomrule
\end{longtable}
At width five the total scanline happens to be sixteen bytes, but only fifteen
belong to the sample interval. A sixteen-byte sample load would consume part of a
neighboring object. At width 512, no scalar tail load is needed after the final group.
These two boundaries explain why neither the full row length nor a nonzero remainder
is the correct condition for admitting another vector.

Use the completed-stage bad-selector recipes in both modes. The malformed selector
is in the last row, so a check that only validates the first row cannot meet the
unchanged-buffer guarantee. Valid Up inputs compare exact wrapping results. The
caller still leaves its framebuffer and final context outputs unchanged; packing
and publication are the next construction boundary.
