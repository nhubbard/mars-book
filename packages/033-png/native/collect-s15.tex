\section{Step 15 Prove that one complete chunk fits}\label{png-collect-s15}
At \texttt{collect\_chunk}, require at least twelve remaining bytes before reading a length. Decode its four big-endian bytes and retain that number at frame offset 80. Reject a length with bit 31 set. Then require length no greater than remaining bytes minus twelve. This subtraction is safe because the twelve-byte minimum was checked first.

The first worked chunk starts at offset eight. Its remaining count is 107, its payload length is 13, and the largest payload that could fit here is 95. The check admits this envelope. It has not yet interpreted the type or validated a checksum. A length of \texttt{0x80000000} is rejected even if represented as a positive 64-bit value.

\paragraph{Observe} Use \texttt{s15-1}; stop before \texttt{collect\_envelope\_ready}. Inspect the state described above, then continue to the next unfinished boundary.

\paragraph{Check} What does the twelve-byte overhead contain?
\begin{itemize}
  \item[A.] The signature and four pixel bytes.
  \item[B.] Length, four type bytes and CRC.
  \item[C.] Only the CRC repeated three times.
\end{itemize}
