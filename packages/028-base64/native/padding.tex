\section{Finish one-byte and two-byte input groups}
When only one input byte remains, form its first two sextets with zero in the missing
byte positions and write two padding characters. For decimal 102, binary
\texttt{01100110}, the sextets are 25 and 32, giving \texttt{Zg==}. Only the high two
bits of sextet 32 carry input; its low four bits are zero. The equal signs occupy
character positions but do not represent additional decoded bytes.

When two input bytes remain, form three sextets and write one padding character.
For bytes 102 and 111, the sextets are 25, 38 and 60, giving \texttt{Zm8=}. The last
sextet carries four input bits and has two zero low bits. A complete three-byte group
uses all four sextets and no padding.

Initialize the missing input-byte registers to zero, and load a second or third byte
only after checking the remaining count. Reading a whole word for a one-byte tail would
access bytes beyond the selected input, even if the surrounding mapped arena happens
to hide the error. Use exact byte loads.

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\MARSCodeLabel{Load only the bytes that remain in the selected input.}
\begin{minted}[samepage=true]{mars-idea}
encode_scalar_group:
    lbu $t0, 0($s0)
    move $t1, $zero
    move $t2, $zero
    sltiu $t3, $s1, 2
    bnez $t3, encode_pack
    nop
    lbu $t1, 1($s0)
    sltiu $t3, $s1, 3
    bnez $t3, encode_pack
    nop
    lbu $t2, 2($s0)
\end{minted}

The output still receives four characters for either short tail. After storing the
available sextets, the two-pad path stores \texttt{=} at position two, then shares the
one-pad path's store at position three. Finish immediately rather than subtracting
three from a remaining count of one or two.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\MARSCodeLabel{Share the final pad store without creating an extra output character.}
\begin{minted}[samepage=true]{mars-idea}
encode_pad_two:
    li $t4, 61
    sb $t4, 2($s2)
encode_pad_one:
    li $t4, 61
    sb $t4, 3($s2)
\end{minted}

Check lengths one, two and three independently before testing longer buffers. Then test
lengths eleven, twelve and thirteen: they cross the first MSA group boundary while
retaining the same tail rules.
