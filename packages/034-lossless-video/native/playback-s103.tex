\section{Step 103 Reconstruct all sixty-four clock bits}
Fill \texttt{vn\_join}. Zero-extend the low half, shift the high half left by thirty-two, then combine the disjoint fields with OR. Return the complete bit pattern in \texttt{\$v1} and status zero in \texttt{\$v0}. The leaf routine retains the return address and saved registers.

The low half can have its sign bit set. For example, halves \texttt{0x80000001} and \texttt{0x12345678} must become \texttt{0x1234567880000001}. A sign-extended low half would fill the upper half with ones before the OR. Shifting left and then logically right by thirty-two discards that unwanted extension.

Use \texttt{s103-1} and stop before \texttt{vn\_join\_ready}.

\paragraph{Observe} The scripted value 1000 is reconstructed exactly. The high-bit test also retains both halves without sign contamination.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\paragraph{Check} Why zero-extend the low half before OR?
\begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.]
  \item To change milliseconds into seconds.
  \item To keep its sign extension from overwriting the high half.
  \item To align the file buffer.
\end{enumerate}
