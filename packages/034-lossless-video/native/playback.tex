\section{Keep presentation time separate from decoding work}
The moving fixture assigns forty milliseconds to each frame, giving eight display
intervals and a total logical duration of 320 milliseconds. Frame six begins at
logical time 240 milliseconds. An unchanged frame still receives its full interval.
A seek chooses pixels by frame index; resuming playback then establishes a new wall-time
anchor for that position.

For a simple player, calculate the next deadline from the initial anchor and the
number of completed intervals. Decode and display the frame, then request only the
positive time remaining before its deadline. Sleeping for a fresh full interval
after every decode would accumulate decoding time into the playback duration. When
work finishes late, continue without a negative sleep and record the lateness.

MARS time service 30 returns wall-clock milliseconds split across two 32-bit halves;
combine those halves as unsigned quantities before doing full-width arithmetic.
Service 32 requests a sleep from its millisecond argument. These host services do
not guarantee exact presentation times. Keep deterministic deadline arithmetic
checks separate from an interactive playback observation, and name any clock-reset
policy explicitly. Simulated instruction counts measure computation, not elapsed
sleep time or UI repaint latency.
