\section{Locate a sample before comparing blocks}
The generator writes one byte per sample in three consecutive planes: Y, Cb and
Cr. Y describes luma; Cb and Cr describe color differences. A raw frame has no
signature, width field or row delimiters. Its interpretation comes from the
accompanying description, not from inspecting the first few bytes.

The luma plane is 176 by 144 samples. Each chroma plane is 88 by 72 samples, giving
4:2:0 sampling. Store rows from top to bottom and samples from left to right, without
padding. These dimensions determine the offsets in Table~\ref{tab:h263-planes}.
The ordering is planar: the first Cb byte follows the last Y byte. It is not an
interleaved sequence of Y, Cb and Cr triples.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{One raw QCIF frame. Offsets and strides are bytes.}\label{tab:h263-planes}
  \tagpdfsetup{table/header-rows={1}}
  \begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
    \toprule
    Plane & Offset  & Width & Height & Stride \\
    \midrule
    Y     & 0       & 176   & 144    & 176    \\
    Cb    & 25\,344 & 88    & 72     & 88     \\
    Cr    & 31\,680 & 88    & 72     & 88     \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

One frame occupies $176\times144+2(88\times72)=38\,016$ bytes. For frame index
$f$, its first byte is at file offset $38\,016f$. Within that frame, a Y sample
at column $x$ and row $y$ is at offset $176y+x$. For the corresponding chroma
region, use coordinates $\lfloor x/2\rfloor$ and $\lfloor y/2\rfloor$, the chroma
stride 88 and the appropriate plane offset. Each byte remains an independent
sample in either target byte order. Byte order matters later when you store
halfword coefficients or emit multi-bit fields.

For example, the Y sample at $(16,32)$ in picture one is at file offset
$38\,016+32\times176+16=43\,664$. Its position within the frame is 5648. Do not
add a chroma offset to that luma address. The first SAD exercise compares the
16-by-16 Y windows at this position in pictures one and zero. Moving between
window rows still advances 176 bytes, although each comparison consumes only
sixteen samples.

Generate the inputs with
\path{construction/h263-fixtures/fixture.py}, using a fresh output directory. For example, from the book source root:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 construction/h263-fixtures/fixture.py \
    --output-dir work/qcif
\end{verbatim}
The output
contains \path{clip.yuv} and \path{clip.json}. The description records offsets,
strides, exact timing and hashes for the file and every frame. The sample pattern
has a stationary tiled background and a moving 32-by-32 square. Its rectangle
edges are aligned to chroma sample boundaries. Picture six repeats picture five,
so the sequence includes a genuine unchanged-picture case.

The generated values stay within the selected nominal ranges: 16--235 for Y and
16--240 for Cb and Cr, with neutral chroma 128. These are input-generation choices.
The SAD entry still accepts every unsigned byte value from zero through 255; its
arithmetic must remain correct outside the pattern's narrower range. QCIF uses a
12:11 sample aspect ratio for display. The raw storage dimensions remain 176 by
144; display scaling must not change the samples supplied to the encoder.
