\section{Separate kernel savings from representation choices}
A shorter record reduces work in the CRC and decoder as well as stored bytes.
That effect is different from replacing a scalar XOR loop with MSA. The following
comparison changes only the XOR entry called by the record encoder. Source images,
greedy run policy, output bytes, CRC, copy and publication remain identical.
Measurements begin at \texttt{video\_encode\_record} and end when it returns;
they exclude fixture preparation, the caller's decoder and presentation time.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Complete record encoding with scalar or MSA XOR, in little-endian mode.}
  \label{tab:video-delta-encode-work}
  \begin{tabular}{l r r r r}
    \toprule
    Pair and sequence & Record bytes & Scalar      & MSA         & Saved instructions \\
    \midrule
    Few, 2            & 40           & 227\,108    & 203\,560    & 23\,548            \\
    Still, 2          & 20           & 225\,593    & 202\,045    & 23\,548            \\
    Tail, 2           & 23           & 225\,823    & 202\,275    & 23\,548            \\
    All change, 2     & 12\,304      & 1\,183\,857 & 1\,160\,309 & 23\,548            \\
    Few, 0            & 12\,304      & 1\,065\,268 & 1\,065\,268 & 0                  \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The full image XOR saves the same 23\,548 simulated instructions whenever it runs.
That is about ten percent of the sparse and unchanged examples, and about two
percent when a measured all-change delta falls back to full encoding. A required
full frame bypasses XOR entirely, so changing the kernel saves nothing there.
These counts are computation measurements, not host-time guarantees or requirements
for equivalent student implementations.

The alternative encoder used in completed verification sometimes chooses additional
full frames and therefore has different bytes and work. Its pixel correctness does
not make those results a controlled kernel comparison. Keep the selected kind,
produced count, input sequence and exact reconstruction beside any performance
claim. A faster path with stale previous pixels is a failed encoder regardless of
its instruction count.
