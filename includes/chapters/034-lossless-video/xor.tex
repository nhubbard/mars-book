\section{Recover a pixel with the same XOR operation}
For a previous word $P$ and a current word $C$, define the delta $D=P\mathbin{\oplus}C$.
Then $P\mathbin{\oplus}D=C$. A bit that did not change contributes zero to the delta.
No color is approximated, and no information is discarded. For valid display words,
the high byte remains zero in both the delta and the reconstructed result.

In the moving fixture, the first pixel of frame zero is hexadecimal
\texttt{0x000061C2}. Frame one changes only its red low bit, producing
\texttt{0x000161C2}. Their XOR is \texttt{0x00010000}; its serialized delta triple
is \texttt{01 00 00}. The next pixel remains \texttt{0x002586E7}, so its delta is
zero and it can be covered by a skip.

A zero delta and a zero-valued pixel are different facts. A black pixel may change
to a nonblack pixel, and two identical nonblack pixels have zero delta. Change
detection therefore compares the whole XOR word with zero. It does not compare the
current pixel with black, count nonzero bytes, or count one change per vector.
