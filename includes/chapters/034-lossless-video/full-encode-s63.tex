\section{Step 63 Extract RGB from numeric source words}
Fill \texttt{ef\_payload}. Restart at the first native source word and begin output at offset sixteen. For each of 4096 pixels, load the numeric word, extract red with a sixteen-bit right shift and green with an eight-bit right shift, and store their low bytes followed by the word's low blue byte. Advance the source by four and output by three. The earlier high-byte validation means this pass needs no new color failure path.

Use \texttt{s63-1} and stop before \texttt{ef\_payload\_ready}.

\paragraph{Observe} At \texttt{ef\_payload\_ready}, the output begins \texttt{12 34 56 12 35 57} and ends \texttt{02 3A A9}. All 12\,288 payload bytes exist. Checksum bytes remain at their original values.

\paragraph{Check} Why extract numeric channels instead of copying three memory bytes?
\begin{enumerate}[label=\Alph*.]
\item Native word byte order differs from the RGB wire order.
\item Every color channel is a native doubleword.
\item All source memory bytes are already in RGB order.
\end{enumerate}
