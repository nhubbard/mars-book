\section{Cancel a component with complete periodic windows}
The listening fixture uses eight coefficients 4096, so each rounded result is the
mean of eight input samples with the specified tie rule. At 16000 samples per second,
the high component repeats \(0,12000,0,-12000\). Four samples span one 4 kHz period.
Every eight-sample window contains two full periods whose integer sum is zero.
The filtered high-only samples are therefore exactly zero, not merely quieter.

The low component is a period-sixty-four integer triangle with amplitude 6000. For
phase \(u\) reduced to zero through sixty-three, it is
\[
  L(u)=375\begin{cases}
    u    & u<16,        \\
    32-u & 16\leq u<48, \\
    u-64 & 48\leq u<64.
  \end{cases}
\]
Its fundamental is 250 Hz at this rate; the triangle is not a pure sine. The mixed
input adds the high component to this triangle sample by sample. Since the high
component contributes zero to every complete window, its filtered mix is byte-identical
to the filtered low-only signal. That is not the original low input: the moving
average changes that component's waveform and delay too.

The renderer supplies seven samples from before time zero using the same periodic
formula. Zero history would give a valid but different startup transient. The kernel
never guesses a missing past. It receives explicit history for each sixty-four-output
call and is reused for five hundred calls per scene. Three scenes give 1500 calls
per implementation, with all output samples and ownership checked.

The companion files contain two seconds each of separate mono input and filtered
tracks for low, high and mixed scenes. The render process preserves kernel amplitudes
and applies no normalization. All six files are identical between the scalar and MSA
implementations. A live playback device has not been tested. Use the integer formulas,
window trace and waveform as an alternative to audio playback.
