\section{Step 11 Compute the row-major cell
address}\label{007-bresenham-line-s11-step-11-compute-the-row-major-cell-address}

Multiplying y by eight gives its row offset in cells. Adding x selects a
cell within that row. Shifting by two converts the cell index to a byte
offset; \texttt{daddu} then adds the full-width base pointer.

\subsection{Write or
inspect}\label{007-bresenham-line-s11-write-or-inspect}

Append the address calculation.

\MARSCodeLabel{MIPS assembly}
\begin{minted}{mars-idea}
    sll $t0, $s1, 3
    addu $t0, $t0, $s0
    sll $t0, $t0, 2
    daddu $t0, $t8, $t0
\end{minted}

\subsection{Expected
observation}\label{007-bresenham-line-s11-expected-observation}

For (1,1), the cell index is nine and the address is 0x10010024.

\subsection{Check your
understanding}\label{007-bresenham-line-s11-check-your-understanding}

Why shift the cell index left by two?

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{A} To double the error for a branch.
\item
  \textbf{B} Each framebuffer cell occupies four bytes.
\item
  \textbf{C} The board has two rows.
\end{itemize}
