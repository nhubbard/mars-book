\section{Canonical direction resolves a reversal
ambiguity}\label{007-bresenham-line-p02-canonical-direction-resolves-a-reversal-ambiguity}

Consider (0, 0) to (2, 1). At x = 1 the ideal y is one-half. Choosing y
= 1 and choosing y = 0 are equally close to the geometric segment. A
walk that resolves ties forward can make opposite choices when its
endpoints are reversed.

The assignment orders endpoints by x, then by y when x is equal. A
reversed call therefore produces the same internal start and end.
Forward at a halfway point now has a stable meaning. For the example,
the middle cell is (1, 1) in either supplied order. A different halfway
convention is a different valid design, but would need a different
published contract.

Normalization does not change the input words. It exchanges the two
working coordinate pairs in registers. It also reduces control flow: x
never needs a negative step in the canonical walk, while y can still
increase or decrease.
