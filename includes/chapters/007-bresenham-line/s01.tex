\section{Step 1 Choose cells rather than continuous
positions}\label{007-bresenham-line-s01-step-1-choose-cells-rather-than-continuous-positions}

A geometric segment passes through continuous coordinates, but the
framebuffer holds discrete cells. The assignment chooses one cell for
every major-axis position, including both endpoints.

\subsection{Write or
inspect}\label{007-bresenham-line-s01-write-or-inspect}

Compare the default segment with the worked grid.

\subsection{Expected
observation}\label{007-bresenham-line-s01-expected-observation}

The default six cells are (1,1), (2,2), (3,2), (4,3), (5,3), (6,4).

\subsection{Check your
understanding}\label{007-bresenham-line-s01-check-your-understanding}

Why does the default line have six cells?

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{A} Its major-axis difference is five and both endpoints are
  included.
\item
  \textbf{B} Every line always has six cells.
\item
  \textbf{C} Its RGB word contains six hexadecimal digits.
\end{itemize}
