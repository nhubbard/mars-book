\section{Freeze the decision input for two independent
tests}\label{007-bresenham-line-p07-freeze-the-decision-input-for-two-independent-tests}

An update can change x, y, or both. Compute e2 = 2 \ensuremath{\times} err once before
either test. If e2 \ensuremath{\geq} dy, take an x step and add dy to the running error.
Independently, if e2 \ensuremath{\leq} dx, take a y step and add dx. Both conditions can
be true, producing diagonal progress.

They are not an if/else pair. The first default update starts with err =
2 and e2 = 4. Since four is at least negative three, x advances and err
becomes negative one. Since the original four is no greater than five, y
also advances and err becomes four. The new point is (2, 2).

Recomputing e2 after the x update would compare a different decision
state in the second test. The saved temporary is therefore part of the
algorithm\textquotesingle s dependency structure. Replacing it with the
updated error can change the selected cells even though every individual
addition still executes accurately.
