\section{Step 18 Verify the whole
  result}\label{003-eight-byte-sprite-s18-step-18-verify-the-whole-result}

A symmetric image can hide a reversal error. The public checks use
asymmetric and walking-bit rows. The private checks cover additional
patterns and poisoned destinations. Comparing every output word also
catches a wrong row stride or an incomplete black background.

\subsection{Write or
  inspect}\label{003-eight-byte-sprite-s18-write-or-inspect}

Run the public checks and explain the first-row reversal with the trace
table. Inspect frame\_guard and rows\_guard in memory.

\subsection{Expected
  observation}\label{003-eight-byte-sprite-s18-expected-observation}

The expected final state includes all 128 pixel words, eight reversed
bytes, unchanged source bytes, preserved canaries, silence, and normal
exit.

\subsection{Check your
  understanding}\label{003-eight-byte-sprite-s18-check-your-understanding}

Why include an asymmetric source row in a mirror test?

\begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
        \textbf{A} Symmetric rows are invalid input.
  \item
        \textbf{B} The simulator cannot reverse a zero byte.
  \item
        \textbf{C} Its expected mirror differs visibly and numerically from
        the original.
\end{itemize}
