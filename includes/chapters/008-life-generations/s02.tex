\section{Step 2 Keep one generation stable while computing the
  next}\label{008-life-generations-s02-step-2-keep-one-generation-stable-while-computing-the-next}

A synchronous update reads every neighbor from the old generation.
Replacing a source cell early can change the inputs seen by later cells.
A second board separates those reads from the new writes.

\subsection{Write or
  inspect}\label{008-life-generations-s02-write-or-inspect}

Compare the synchronous and in-place worked example.

\subsection{Expected
  observation}\label{008-life-generations-s02-expected-observation}

Changing traversal order must not change a completed generation.

\subsection{Check your
  understanding}\label{008-life-generations-s02-check-your-understanding}

Why use a separate destination?

\begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
        \textbf{A} To keep earlier writes from changing later neighbor reads.
  \item
        \textbf{B} To double every neighbor count.
  \item
        \textbf{C} To change the rule on alternate rows.
\end{itemize}
