\section{Make the boundary visible in
  memory}\label{008-life-generations-p02-make-the-boundary-visible-in-memory}

An 8 by 8 logical board is stored inside a 10 by 10 byte allocation. Its
outer row and column on each side form a halo: a band of permanently
dead cells around the interior. Logical cell (0,0) is physical row one,
column one, at byte offset eleven.

The halo lets every interior cell use the same eight offsets. At the
top-left corner, loads above or to the left reach halo bytes rather than
invalid memory. At the lower-right corner, the largest neighbor offset
reaches the allocation\textquotesingle s final byte. No special corner
branch is needed in the counting body.

The halo is not merely a performance trick. Keeping it zero at every
step defines the external world as dead. An infinite board could create
off-board cells that later influence the visible region, and a
wraparound board would connect opposite edges. Neither model is the
finite system specified here.
