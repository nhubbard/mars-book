\section{Step 10 Map zero and one into color
words}\label{008-life-generations-s10-step-10-map-zero-and-one-into-color-words}

For binary c, \texttt{subu} zero,c gives zero when dead and all-one bits
when alive. AND with 0x00ffffff produces black or white. The full word
store clears an old live pixel when its new state is dead.

\subsection{Write or
inspect}\label{008-life-generations-s10-write-or-inspect}

Append the render-cell body.

\MARSCodeLabel{MIPS assembly}
\begin{minted}{mars-idea}
    lbu $t4, 0($t0)
    subu $t4, $zero, $t4
    and $t4, $t4, $t5
    sw $t4, 0($t1)
    daddiu $t0, $t0, 1
    daddiu $t1, $t1, 4
\end{minted}

\subsection{Expected
observation}\label{008-life-generations-s10-expected-observation}

The first default cell is black; no other frame cell has been rendered
at this partial stage.

\subsection{Check your
understanding}\label{008-life-generations-s10-check-your-understanding}

Why store zero for a dead cell?

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{A} Zero terminates every program.
\item
  \textbf{B} A previous live pixel must be erased.
\item
  \textbf{C} Dead cells have no framebuffer address.
\end{itemize}
