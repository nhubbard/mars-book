\section{Step 13 Reject coordinates outside the grid}\label{maze-s13}

Adding a direction to a row or column can produce minus one or eight. A valid byte address alone cannot prove that the cell is a geometric neighbor.

\subsection{Write or inspect}

Append the direction loads and coordinate bounds. The direction table uses signed byte loads because minus one is meaningful.

\MARSCodeLabel{MIPS assembly}
\begin{minted}{mars-idea}
    la $t0, dx
    daddu $t0, $t0, $a0
    lb $t1, 0($t0)
    addu $t1, $t1, $a1
    sltiu $t2, $t1, 8
    beq $t2, $zero, next_neighbor
    nop
    la $t0, dy
    daddu $t0, $t0, $a0
    lb $t2, 0($t0)
    addu $t2, $t2, $a2
    sltiu $t3, $t2, 8
    beq $t3, $zero, next_neighbor
    nop
    sll $t3, $t2, 3
    addu $t3, $t3, $t1
\end{minted}

\subsection{Expected observation}

The north neighbor of cell zero is rejected before any maze array load. Only in-range coordinates are converted back into a cell ID.

\subsection{Check your understanding}

Why load a direction byte with a signed load?

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A} The table contains negative displacements.
\item \textbf{B} All cell IDs are negative.
\item \textbf{C} Byte loads must always sign extend.
\end{itemize}
