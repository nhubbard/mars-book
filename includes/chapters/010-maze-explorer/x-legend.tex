\section{Read the frame without relying on color names}\label{maze-x-legend}
\begin{longtable}{@{}p{32mm}p{31mm}p{95mm}@{}}
  \toprule
  Classification   & RGB word          & Meaning and priority                      \\\midrule\endhead
  Wall, dark       & \texttt{0x202020} & Wall byte is 1; overrides all work flags. \\
  Route, blue      & \texttt{0x0072b2} & Open cell with path byte 1.               \\
  Unseen, white    & \texttt{0xffffff} & Open cell with path 0 and state 0.        \\
  Frontier, orange & \texttt{0xe69f00} & Open cell with path 0 and state 1.        \\
  Processed, green & \texttt{0x009e73} & Open cell with path 0 and state 2.        \\
  \bottomrule
\end{longtable}
The frame is a rendering of the arrays. The arrays and this legend supply the text
representation when colors are hard to distinguish. In the final small-room frame,
IDs 0, 1, and 9 are route cells, ID 8 is processed, and all remaining IDs are walls.
