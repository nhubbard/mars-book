\section{The public work arrays}\label{maze-x-storage}
All six byte arrays below have 64 entries and four-byte-aligned bases. Their elements
are still bytes; alignment does not change the stride. The frame contains 64 words.
\begin{longtable}{@{}p{28mm}p{43mm}p{87mm}@{}}
  \toprule
  Array             & Per-cell meaning       & Values and initialization                                    \\\midrule\endhead
  \texttt{walls}    & Input occupancy        & 0 open, 1 wall; never written by execution.                  \\
  \texttt{states}   & Discovery status       & 0 unseen, 1 frontier, 2 processed; initialize to 0.          \\
  \texttt{parents}  & First predecessor      & Cell ID 0 through 63, or 255; initialize to 255.             \\
  \texttt{distance} & Shortest edge count    & Finite 0 through 63, or 255; initialize to 255.              \\
  \texttt{queue}    & Discovery history      & Cell IDs in the used prefix; initialize the full array to 0. \\
  \texttt{path}     & Final route membership & 0 or 1; initialize to 0.                                     \\
  \bottomrule
\end{longtable}
The six arrays include the input maze. Five are working arrays initialized by the program.
Words \texttt{head}, \texttt{tail}, \texttt{current}, \texttt{phase}, \texttt{result},
and \texttt{length} describe progress. These words require word loads and stores.
