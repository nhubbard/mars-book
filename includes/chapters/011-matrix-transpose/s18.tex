\section{Step 18 Reuse the one-cell copy inside a tile}\label{transpose-s18}

The coordinate formula has selected the first cell of a tile row. The cell body now has the same structure as the ordinary version: load one word, store it, advance the source by one word and the destination by one output row. Only the surrounding bounds have changed.

\subsection{Write or inspect}
Append the tiled cell body. This partial function copies one word for a nonempty matrix, because its repetition branches are still absent.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\MARSCodeLabel{MIPS assembly}
\begin{minted}[samepage=true]{mars-idea}
next_tiled_column:
    lw $t8, 0($t6)
    sw $t8, 0($t7)
    daddiu $t6, $t6, 4
    daddu $t7, $t7, $v1
\end{minted}

\subsection{Expected observation}
The first word reaches output word zero. Both pointers retain their full widths, and the source allocation is unchanged.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\subsection{Check your understanding}
Why can the cell-copy logic be reused?

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A} Every cell still obeys the same transpose address mapping.
\item \textbf{B} Every tile begins at address zero.
\item \textbf{C} All tile rows are packed into four words.
\end{itemize}
