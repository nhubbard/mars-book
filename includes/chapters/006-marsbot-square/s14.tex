\section{Step 14 Stop at the
  origin}\label{006-marsbot-square-s14-step-14-stop-at-the-origin}

The final loop polls y against zero. Its stop makes the return explicit
instead of relying on the board boundary to hold a still-enabled robot
in place.

\subsection{Write or
  inspect}\label{006-marsbot-square-s14-write-or-inspect}

Append the north polling loop.

\MARSCodeLabel{MIPS assembly}
\begin{minted}{mars-idea}
north_wait:
    lw $t2, 0x40($s0)
    bne $t2, $zero, north_wait
    nop
    sw $zero, 0x50($s0)
north_stopped:
\end{minted}

\subsection{Expected
  observation}\label{006-marsbot-square-s14-expected-observation}

The trail is closed at (0, 0), heading zero, movement disabled, pen
still down.

\subsection{Check your
  understanding}\label{006-marsbot-square-s14-check-your-understanding}

Why explicitly stop at the boundary?

\begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
        \textbf{A} A boundary automatically clears every control.
  \item
        \textbf{B} The board wraps around if the pen is down.
  \item
        \textbf{C} Clamping position does not disable movement.
\end{itemize}
