\section{Step 15 Compare an equivalent
implementation}\label{004-bit-inspector-s15-step-15-compare-an-equivalent-implementation}

A 64-bit left shift by 32 followed by a logical right shift by 32
isolates the low word. A word shift by zero can normalize a wide sum to
a sign-extended word result. These operations give an alternative under
the same interface.

\subsection{Write or
inspect}\label{004-bit-inspector-s15-write-or-inspect}

Study the alternative fixture, then implement an equivalent solution in
a separate copy. The wide shift instructions below use immediate zero
but their 32 suffix adds 32 to the shift distance.

\MARSCodeLabel{MIPS assembly}
\begin{minted}{mars-idea}
    dsll32 $s1, $s0, 0
    dsrl32 $s1, $s1, 0
\end{minted}

\subsection{Expected
observation}\label{004-bit-inspector-s15-expected-observation}

The first operation moves the low word into the high half and discards
the former high half. The logical right shift returns that word with
zeroes above it.

\subsection{Check your
understanding}\label{004-bit-inspector-s15-check-your-understanding}

What is the actual shift distance in dsrl32 with immediate zero?

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{A} 32 bits.
\item
  \textbf{B} Zero bits.
\item
  \textbf{C} 64 bits.
\end{itemize}
