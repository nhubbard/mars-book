\small
\begin{enumerate}
\item Only the sample initializer changed, from $-1$ to 2147483647, or \texttt{0x7fffffff}. Both loads initially produce the same positive value because bit 31 is clear. The program is now paused after all result stores, before its report setup.
\item \texttt{addiu} produces low word \texttt{80000000} and sign-extends that word into \texttt{\$s2}: \texttt{0xffffffff80000000}, or $-2147483648$. This instruction does not trap on the signed overflow; the letter \texttt{u} does not make its result zero-extended.
\item \texttt{daddiu} performs the addition at 64-bit width. \texttt{\$s3}=\texttt{0x0000000080000000}=2147483648. The low 32 bits agree with the word result; their upper halves and signed values differ.
\item The input at \texttt{0x10010000} remains \texttt{ff ff ff 7f}. The word-result field at \texttt{0x10010018} is \texttt{00 00 00 80 ff ff ff ff}; the wide-result field at \texttt{0x10010020} is \texttt{00 00 00 80 00 00 00 00}. The input and result guards remain intact, and the final four displayed bytes begin the message text.
\end{enumerate}
