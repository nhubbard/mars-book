\section{Zero extension preserves an unsigned
value}\label{004-bit-inspector-p04-zero-extension-preserves-an-unsigned-value}

Zero extension puts zeroes in the upper half while keeping the low word.
For a word with bit 31 clear, the result equals the sign-extended value.
For a word with bit 31 set, the two full-register results differ by
2\^{}32.

Thus ffffffff becomes 00000000ffffffff under \texttt{lwu}, representing
4294967295. The \texttt{lw} result from those same bytes is \ensuremath{-}1. Neither
load changes memory; the instruction determines the widening rule
applied to the loaded pattern.

This gives a useful way to diagnose a result. If the low word matches
but the upper half is wrong, inspect the operation that introduced or
normalized the value. Looking only at a decimal word report can conceal
the distinction.
