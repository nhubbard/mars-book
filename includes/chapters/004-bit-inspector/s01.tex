\section{Step 1 Identify the input
  pattern}\label{004-bit-inspector-s01-step-1-identify-the-input-pattern}

The input is the word stored at sample, not a line typed into the
console. A word has 32 bits. Its signed interpretation and unsigned
interpretation can differ even though its stored bits remain the same.
The default -1 has all 32 bits set.

\subsection{Write or
  inspect}\label{004-bit-inspector-s01-write-or-inspect}

Inspect the supplied sample and result fields. Predict its four output
lines and identify which parts depend on a signed interpretation.

\subsection{Expected
  observation}\label{004-bit-inspector-s01-expected-observation}

The same ffffffff pattern is signed -1 and unsigned 4294967295. Hex and
binary describe the pattern directly.

\subsection{Check your
  understanding}\label{004-bit-inspector-s01-check-your-understanding}

Does switching from signed to unsigned interpretation change the stored
word?

\begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
        \textbf{A} Yes; every bit is complemented.
  \item
        \textbf{B} Yes; the word grows automatically to eight bytes.
  \item
        \textbf{C} No; it changes the numerical interpretation of the same
        bits.
\end{itemize}
