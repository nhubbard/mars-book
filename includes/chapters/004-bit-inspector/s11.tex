\section{Step 11 Print
hexadecimal}\label{004-bit-inspector-s11-step-11-print-hexadecimal}

Service 34 prints 0x and eight hexadecimal digits for the low word. A
hexadecimal digit represents four bits, so eight digits describe all 32
word bits. Leading zeroes preserve that fixed width.

\subsection{Write or
inspect}\label{004-bit-inspector-s11-write-or-inspect}

Append the hexadecimal line.

\MARSCodeLabel{MIPS assembly}
\begin{minted}{mars-idea}
    la $a0, hex_msg
    li $v0, 4
    syscall
    move $a0, $s0
    li $v0, 34
    syscall
    la $a0, newline
    li $v0, 4
    syscall
hex_printed:
\end{minted}

\subsection{Expected
observation}\label{004-bit-inspector-s11-expected-observation}

The third line is hex: 0xffffffff. With a zero sample it would be hex:
0x00000000.

\subsection{Check your
understanding}\label{004-bit-inspector-s11-check-your-understanding}

How many word bits does one hexadecimal digit represent?

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{A} Sixteen.
\item
  \textbf{B} Four.
\item
  \textbf{C} Eight.
\end{itemize}
