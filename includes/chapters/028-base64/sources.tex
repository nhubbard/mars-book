\section{Sources and draft status}
The standard alphabet, padding conventions, canonical pad-bit discussion and short
anchors follow \href{https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4648.html}{RFC 4648}, sections
3, 4 and 10. The strict acceptance language, fixed input bounds, statuses, capacities,
allowed aliases and kernel footprints are local teaching contracts. The independent
host comparison uses the basic \href{https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/25/docs/api/java.base/java/util/Base64.html}{JDK Base64 API}
with an explicit canonical re-encoding check. MSA shifts, comparisons, byte transfers
and destructive selectors were checked against the current MARS implementation.

This directly edited native foundation explains the scalar and vector calculations and
their observation boundaries. Two private whole-program implementations pass 1124 cases
each at ordinary addresses and again above 4 GiB, for 4496 positive executions.
Twenty-four deliberately faulty variants are rejected. A separate Python integer model
checks the worked bit splits, every selector, alphabet values, canonical examples and
length bounds. These are finite functional checks, not an exhaustive-equivalence or
student-usability assessment.

Cumulative construction steps, public starter and checks, quizzes and separate student
and instructor exports remain next. Actual automated light-theme IDE figures, remote
progression and independent accessibility review remain unfinished. This foundation is
not yet included in main.tex. The author's requested substantial exposition and length
rewrite remains deferred until all thirty-five chapter drafts exist.
