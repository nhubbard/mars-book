\section{Repeat observations at the boundary cases}
Use each recipe with the cumulative source through its named step. A recipe changes
inputs and controls; it does not fill a missing region. Stop at its caller checkpoint
before continuing through the return and observing the stored status.

After step 2, compare \texttt{s02-empty}, \texttt{s02-limit} and
\texttt{s02-capacity}. The first has zero count and capacity. The second sets the
unsigned count to \(2^{63}\), which must fail the bound even though a signed comparison
would interpret it as negative. The third supplies \texttt{Man} with capacity three,
one byte short. These decisions precede source reads. After step 8, use
\texttt{s08-two} for \texttt{fo}; compare its single pad with the two pads in the
one-byte recipe.

After step 11, \texttt{s11-lower} selects lowercase \texttt{z}. After step 12,
\texttt{s12-slash} and \texttt{s12-invalid} distinguish slash from \texttt{@}.
Inspect the numerical classifier return before the caller translates it into status.
A valid sextet and a whole-function status have different meanings.

After step 15, \texttt{s15-badbits} supplies \texttt{Zm9=}; after step 16,
\texttt{s16-badbits} supplies \texttt{Zh==}. Both strings have a recognizable alphabet
and pad shape, but their unused bits are nonzero. Their checkpoints precede the
malformed-input return. Inspect the bytes there, then continue to see status two.

After step 18, use \texttt{s18-empty}, \texttt{s18-limit} and
\texttt{s18-shape}. They test empty input, the same unsigned \(2^{63}\) count and
three-character \texttt{AAA}. After step 24, \texttt{s24-one} and
\texttt{s24-two} decode \texttt{Zg==} and \texttt{Zm8=}. Check the exact one- and
two-byte writes and the untouched following byte.

After step 41, \texttt{s41-inplace} repeats the twenty-five-byte binary example with
decoded output at the same address as its text input. Follow the read cursor and the
write cursor independently. A sixteen-character group must be read before its twelve
output bytes replace any of those characters.
