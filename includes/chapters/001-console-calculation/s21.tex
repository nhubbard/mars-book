\section{Step 21 Trace before trusting the
  result}\label{001-console-calculation-s21-step-21-trace-before-trusting-the-result}

A breakpoint pauses before its instruction executes. Step Over follows
source-level execution; pseudo-instructions may expand into multiple
machine instructions. The two arithmetic lines here are basic
instructions and give direct state observations.

\subsection{Write or
  inspect}\label{001-console-calculation-s21-write-or-inspect}

Set a breakpoint on addu below calculate. Debug with input 20. Inspect
\$v0 before the addition, then \$t0 after each of the two arithmetic
steps. Resume to termination.

\subsection{Expected
  observation}\label{001-console-calculation-s21-expected-observation}

Before addu: \$v0 = 20. After addu: \$t0 = 40. After addiu: \$t0 = 41.
In hexadecimal these are 0x14, 0x28, and 0x29.

\subsection{Check your
  understanding}\label{001-console-calculation-s21-check-your-understanding}

When stopped at the breakpoint on addu, has that instruction executed?

\begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
        \textbf{A} It executes twice whenever a breakpoint is present.
  \item
        \textbf{B} No; it is the next instruction to execute.
  \item
        \textbf{C} Yes; the breakpoint always stops after it.
\end{itemize}
