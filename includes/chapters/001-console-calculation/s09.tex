\section{Step 9 Double the
value}\label{001-console-calculation-s09-step-9-double-the-value}

The first operand of addu is its destination. The next two operands are
source registers. Reading \$v0 twice adds x to itself and writes 2 \ensuremath{\times} x
into \$t0. addu performs word arithmetic and sign-extends the 32-bit
result into the 64-bit register. It does not trap on signed overflow;
our input bounds keep the demonstrated sums in range.

\subsection{Write or
inspect}\label{001-console-calculation-s09-write-or-inspect}

Append the addition. This is the first arithmetic instruction.

\MARSCodeLabel{MIPS assembly}
\begin{minted}{mars-idea}
calculate:
    addu $t0, $v0, $v0
\end{minted}

\subsection{Expected
observation}\label{001-console-calculation-s09-expected-observation}

Immediately after this instruction, input 20 gives \$t0 = 40. Input -3
gives \$t0 = -6.

\subsection{Check your
understanding}\label{001-console-calculation-s09-check-your-understanding}

For input -3, what is \$t0 after this addition?

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{A} 6, because the u forces a positive result.
\item
  \textbf{B} -6.
\item
  \textbf{C} -3, because a source cannot appear twice.
\end{itemize}
