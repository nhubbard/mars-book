\section{Step 77 Restore and store one sample}
Fill \texttt{row\_store}. Load the current residual as an unsigned byte, add the predictor in \texttt{\$t4}, and store the low byte at the destination cursor. The byte store supplies reduction modulo 256. Advance destination and upper cursors by one; the supplied continuation tests the exclusive end.

At the first worked Up sample, residual five plus predictor ten produces 15. At the checkpoint, that byte has been stored and both cursors have advanced once. The row selector remains two. Earlier None samples are still 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, because that row required no arithmetic.

Wrapping differs from saturation. A residual of ten above an upper sample of 250 must produce four, since 260 modulo 256 is four. Clamping to 255 would be a different image. The public two-row Up fixtures make this difference visible across both a scalar tail and, later, vector groups.
