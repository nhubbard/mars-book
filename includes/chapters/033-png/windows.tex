\section{Keep counts beside cursors}
Represent a readable window with a full-width address and an unsigned remaining-byte
count. Before reading a chunk envelope, require at least twelve bytes: four for its
length, four for its type and four for its CRC. Then compare the decoded payload
length with \emph{remaining minus twelve}. This ordering avoids forming an unchecked
\texttt{length + 12}. Check the format's length limit as well as the supplied window.
Only after these checks may the parser read the payload or the stored CRC.

Use 64-bit address arithmetic even when a dimension or byte count fits in 32 bits.
A small buffer can live above 4 GiB. Saving its pointer with a word store, or advancing
it with a 32-bit addition, can destroy its high bits. Byte samples are unsigned:
\texttt{lbu} loads the values 128 through 255 without sign extension. Use a doubleword
save for a retained address, and distinguish the byte count from the address to which
it will be added. These choices also apply to private workspace and saved stack state.

The size bounds make the largest row $3\times512=1536$ sample bytes. Its filter prefix
raises that to 1537, and 512 such rows occupy 786944 bytes. Derive the required size
after validating dimensions; require sufficient caller storage before writing. A
compressed input bound alone does not bound decompressed output closely enough.
