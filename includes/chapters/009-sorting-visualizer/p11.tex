\section{Branch direction is distinct from branch
  prediction}\label{009-sorting-visualizer-p11-branch-direction-is-distinct-from-branch-prediction}

The counter uses the core\textquotesingle s branch-decision metadata.
Taken and not-taken outcomes both contribute to the total. An
always-taken \texttt{beq} is still a branch decision. A \texttt{jal}, j,
or \texttt{jr} is a jump rather than a branch under this definition, and
its delay-slot \texttt{nop} contributes an instruction without adding a
branch.

The measured selection range executes 115 branches on each of the three
eight-item workloads. Its scan structure is fixed, but outcome
directions vary. Sorted input has 79 taken and 36 not taken; reversed
input has 59 and 56. Those counts describe decisions, not
mispredictions, pipeline flushes, or physical instruction latency.

Forward counters do not subtract events when backstep undoes state.
Re-executing an instruction adds a new forward event, matching the
IDE\textquotesingle s counting convention. The reported measurements use
fresh runs with no replay. Correctness cases do not require a student
implementation to reproduce the reference\textquotesingle s instruction
or branch totals.
