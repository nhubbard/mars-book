\section{Step 14 Limit the \texttt{f4} upper bound}\label{utf8-s14}

For four-byte leads other than \texttt{f0}, distinguish \texttt{f4}. Its next byte can be at most decimal 143, hexadecimal \texttt{8f}. Leads \texttt{f1} through \texttt{f3} retain ordinary bounds. Every accepted lead jumps past the other class setup.

\subsection{Write or inspect}
Append these instructions in the \texttt{four\_high} region. Keep the scaffold labels and return stub. Intermediate values do not yet establish the final interface.

\subsection{Expected observation}
The greatest accepted four-byte scalar encoding is \texttt{f4} \texttt{8f} \texttt{bf} \texttt{bf}.

\subsection{Check your understanding}
Why reject \texttt{f4} \texttt{90} before reading two more bytes?

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A} No completion can stay within the scalar limit.
\item \textbf{B} The byte \texttt{90} is never a continuation.
\item \textbf{C} The sequence must use five bytes.
\end{itemize}
