\section{Step 11 Dispatch a three-byte lead}\label{utf8-s11}

After excluding the two-byte class, compare with 240, hexadecimal \texttt{f0}. Candidates below it are \texttt{e0} through \texttt{ef} and branch to the two-continuation setup. That setup will distinguish \texttt{e0} and \texttt{ed} from the ordinary three-byte leads.

\subsection{Write or inspect}
Append these instructions in the \texttt{three\_dispatch} region. Keep the scaffold labels and return stub. Intermediate values do not yet establish the final interface.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\MARSCodeLabel{MIPS assembly}
\begin{minted}[samepage=true]{mars-idea}
    sltiu $t9, $t8, 240
    bne $t9, $zero, scan_three
    nop
\end{minted}

\subsection{Expected observation}
The number of required bytes and the first-tail bounds are separate decisions.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\subsection{Check your understanding}
Which three-byte leads need special next-byte bounds?

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A} \texttt{e0} and \texttt{ed}.
\item \textbf{B} \texttt{e1} and \texttt{ef}.
\item \textbf{C} Every lead uses the same special bound.
\end{itemize}
