\section{Step 22 Separate zero and unit moduli}\label{rsa-s22}

Read the modulus before other caller objects. Reject zero; recognize one across all four limbs and use the supplied zero-result path. For every larger modulus, continue toward comparison. This makes the private integer one a valid residue wherever the bit loop needs it.

Replace only the body of region \texttt{reduce\_guard}, retaining its BEGIN and END comments.

\Needspace{25\baselineskip}
\MARSCodeLabel{MIPS assembly}
\begin{minted}[samepage=true]{mars-idea}
    lwu $t0, 0($a1)
    lwu $t1, 4($a1)
    lwu $t2, 8($a1)
    lwu $t3, 12($a1)
    or $t0, $t0, $t1
    or $t0, $t0, $t2
    or $t0, $t0, $t3
    beqz $t0, rsa_invalid
    nop
    lwu $t0, 0($a1)
    li $t1, 1
    bne $t0, $t1, reduce_begin_compare
    nop
    lwu $t0, 4($a1)
    lwu $t1, 8($a1)
    lwu $t2, 12($a1)
    or $t0, $t0, $t1
    or $t0, $t0, $t2
    beqz $t0, reduce_modulus_one
    nop
\end{minted}

Use recipe \texttt{s22-1} with the cumulative source through this edit. Stop at \texttt{reduce\_begin\_compare} before continuing to the temporary or normal return.

\subsection{Expected observation}
A zero modulus returns one; a unit modulus writes zero; larger moduli reach comparison.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\subsection{Check your understanding}
Why handle modulus one before the bit loop?

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A} The object has no readable low limb.
  \item \textbf{B} The private integer one would violate that loop's addition precondition.
  \item \textbf{C} The exponent becomes negative for modulus one.
\end{itemize}
