\small
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
\item The stop at \texttt{after\_add\_limbs} follows all four sum stores. The comparison and conditional subtraction have not executed.
\item \texttt{t0 = 16} is the completed byte cursor; \texttt{t1 = 1} is the carry beyond bit 127. That carry is outside the stored four-limb object.
\item The left input is $2^{128}-160$, beginning \texttt{60 ff ff ff}. All sixteen input bytes form one unsigned integer.
\item The right input is the same residue. Both operands are below $N=2^{128}-159$, satisfying the modular-add interface.
\item The modulus begins \texttt{61 ff ff ff}. Comparing just the stored low sum against these four limbs would incorrectly choose to keep the sum.
\item The stored sum begins \texttt{c0 fe ff ff} and equals $2^{128}-320$. Its separate carry makes the full total larger than $N$. Subtracting once gives $2^{128}-161$; the output is still intermediate at this stop.
\end{enumerate}
