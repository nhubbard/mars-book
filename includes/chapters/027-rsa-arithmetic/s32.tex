\section{Step 32 Transfer the multiplier across 64-bit halves}\label{rsa-s32}

Shift the private multiplier logically. Transfer the low bit of its high doubleword into bit 63 of the low doubleword. Test both halves after the shift: a zero low half does not imply that the 128-bit multiplier is zero. If bits remain, the next region will double the addend.

Replace only the body of region \texttt{modproduct\_shift}, retaining its BEGIN and END comments.

\Needspace{17\baselineskip}
\MARSCodeLabel{MIPS assembly}
\begin{minted}[samepage=true]{mars-idea}
mulmod_shift:
    ld $t0, 16($sp)
    ld $t1, 24($sp)
    dsrl $t0, $t0, 1
    dsll $t2, $t1, 63
    or $t0, $t0, $t2
    dsrl $t1, $t1, 1
    sd $t0, 16($sp)
    sd $t1, 24($sp)
    or $t2, $t0, $t1
    beqz $t2, mulmod_done
    nop
\end{minted}

Use recipe \texttt{s32-1} with the cumulative source through this edit. Stop at \texttt{after\_modproduct\_shift} before continuing to the temporary or normal return.

\subsection{Expected observation}
The first shift changes 11 to five; the result is 13 and the addend remains 13.

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\subsection{Check your understanding}
Which bit crosses the doubleword boundary?

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A} The high bit of the low half moves upward.
  \item \textbf{B} The sign bit is copied into both halves.
  \item \textbf{C} The old low bit of the high half.
\end{itemize}
