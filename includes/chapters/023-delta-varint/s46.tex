\section{Step 46 Allow a single-byte value without a minimality test}\label{delta-s46}

At \texttt{dec\_terminal}, compare candidate length with one. A one-byte zero payload is valid and can proceed directly to inverse mapping. Longer candidates must prove their terminal payload is nonzero.

\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\subsection{Write and inspect}
Replace the supplied temporary branch and nop in \texttt{d\_single}. Keep surrounding labels and region markers.

\MARSCodeLabel{MIPS assembly}
\begin{minted}[samepage=true]{mars-idea}
    li $t6, 1
    beq $t3, $t6, dec_unmap
    nop
\end{minted}
Use \texttt{s46-1}, \texttt{s46-2} from the companion construction inputs. Pause at \texttt{codec\_done} before caller result storage.

\subsection{Expected observation}
Byte 00 represents one complete zero value; the default two-byte candidate proceeds toward the minimality check.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\subsection{Check your understanding}
Why is a zero terminal payload allowed for a single byte?

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A} Zero itself needs a one-byte representation.
  \item \textbf{B} Every byte with bit seven clear is canonical.
  \item \textbf{C} The input extent supplies a separator.
\end{itemize}
