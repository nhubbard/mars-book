\section{Measure size on data with different change patterns}
The table uses thirty-two readings per dataset, occupying 128 bytes as fixed-width
words. The independent column maps each reading directly through ZigZag and base 128,
without delta subtraction. The delta column applies this chapter's complete transform.
All output bytes are checked against the independent arithmetic oracle.
\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Encoded bytes for deterministic thirty-two-reading datasets.}
  \tagpdfsetup{table/header-rows={1}}
  \begin{tabular}{lrrr}
    \toprule
    Dataset                       & Fixed words & Independent mapped values & Mapped deltas \\
    \midrule
    Steady 1000                   & 128         & 64                        & 33            \\
    Ramp from \(-16\) through 15  & 128         & 32                        & 32            \\
    Timestamps increasing by 1000 & 128         & 160                       & 67            \\
    One spike to 1000000          & 128         & 34                        & 36            \\
    Alternating signed endpoints  & 128         & 160                       & 160           \\
    Seeded random words           & 128         & 159                       & 159           \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The steady sequence needs two bytes for its first value and one for each later zero
delta. The ramp's independently mapped values already fit in one byte, so subtraction
saves no further bytes. The spike creates two large changes, increasing its result from
34 bytes to 36 relative to independent mapping. Alternating endpoints and random words
expand relative to fixed-width storage. A variable-length code is not a guarantee of
compression for every input.

The timestamp dataset begins at 1000000000 and adds 1000 at each position. The spike
is at zero-based position sixteen, with all other values zero. Alternating endpoints
starts at the signed minimum. Random words use initial state \texttt{0x13579bdf},
then \(s\gets1664525s+1013904223\) modulo \(2^{32}\), interpreting each new state
as a signed word. These definitions make the table reproducible.

\begin{MARSExercise}{Choose what a size result means}
  Calculate the byte saving of the steady sequence relative to fixed words. Then compare
  the spike with both baselines. Which comparison would conceal the cost added by delta
  subtraction? Keep input values, sequence length and signed mapping constant.
\end{MARSExercise}
