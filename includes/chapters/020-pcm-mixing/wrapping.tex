\section{Follow the different result of narrowing early}\label{pcm-wrapping}
Storing only the low sixteen bits of an out-of-range sum gives modular arithmetic.
For this input domain, signed wrapping can be written as
\[
  \operatorname{wrap}_{16}(s)=
  \begin{cases}
    s+65536 & s<-32768,              \\
    s       & -32768\leq s\leq32767, \\
    s-65536 & s>32767.
  \end{cases}
\]
A positive sum of 40000 then becomes minus 25536. A negative sum of minus 40000
becomes positive 25536. These changes can cross almost the full signed range as
an input passes the limit. Saturation instead stays at the corresponding endpoint.
The supplied comparison routine keeps this wrapping behavior so its output can be
examined and heard alongside the required mixer.

Saturation itself changes the signal. It bounds the stored codes by flattening
out-of-range peaks; it cannot preserve an excessive exact amplitude. Lowering input
gains before adding can avoid that clipping, but it is a different operation from
this function's prescribed sum. A gain adjustment after clipping cannot recover
the discarded peak values.

Saturating addition is also not associative. With inputs 30000, 10000 and minus
10000, clipping the first pair and then adding the third yields 22767. Clipping
the second pair first and then adding the first yields 30000. A multi-input mixer
needs an explicit accumulation and clipping policy. This chapter's two-input
contract fixes the point at which clipping occurs.
