\section{Read a complete caller result}\label{pcm-caller}
The default caller mixes sixteen samples into \texttt{pcm\_c}, then computes the
wrapping comparison into \texttt{pcm\_d}. The two original inputs are
\texttt{pcm\_a} and \texttt{pcm\_b}. This particular caller uses separate outputs,
while callable checks also exercise exact input/output aliases.

At \texttt{mix\_ready}, the result registers contain processed count sixteen and
clipped count six. Output samples are already stored, but caller words
\texttt{processed}, \texttt{clipped} and \texttt{wrapped\_count} still contain 99.
Continuing copies the first two registers before invoking the comparison routine.
At \texttt{wrap\_ready}, the comparison returns sixteen and zero; its second result
is not the required mixer's clipping count. At \texttt{comparison\_ready}, all
three caller words have been stored.

The first eight samples clip at indices three, four and seven. The second group
clips at indices eight, nine and ten, giving six total. Sample five already equals
the upper endpoint before adding zero, and sample six equals the lower endpoint.
Neither counts as clipped. This separates saturation output from clipping evidence
in one trace.
