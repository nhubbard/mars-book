\section{Treat the audio stream as signed samples}\label{pcm-purpose}
Pulse-code modulation, or PCM, represents a signal as a sequence of sample codes.
This exercise uses linear signed sixteen-bit codes. Zero is the central code;
positive and negative values describe opposite directions of amplitude. Their
numeric range is minus 32768 through 32767. There are 65,536 possible codes, with
one more negative magnitude than positive magnitude.

The two arrays represent separate mono input streams at matching sample times.
Adding their corresponding samples produces one mono output stream. Two inputs
do not by themselves create two-channel stereo. The kernel only mixes already
aligned sample values; file parsing, resampling and channel arrangement belong to
other parts of an audio pipeline.

Sample rate gives the number of sample frames per second per channel. For the
provided mono listening files, one frame contains one two-byte sample. At 16,000
frames per second, 32,000 frames last two seconds. The arithmetic kernel does not
receive a sample rate because a pointwise integer sum does not depend on playback
timing. The WAV writer records timing and channel information separately.
