\section{Keep accumulator slots separate from shared state}\label{expression-slots}
The term and expression frames use byte offset zero for their current left value,
eight for a pending operator, sixteen for its byte offset, and forty for their
return address. Both frames occupy 48 bytes. Loading the right operand may require
another invocation of the same rules, but its frame occupies different addresses.
Do not replace those slots with one shared accumulator register.

The primary and unary frames occupy 32 bytes and keep their return address at byte
24. A primary saves a grouped value at zero while checking the close. A unary call
saves its sign position at zero and sign byte at eight. These are local meanings:
a word at byte zero of a unary frame is not the same object as a word at byte zero
of an expression frame.

The supplied frame saves use doublewords. The values stored there include return
addresses and full-register operands. Restoration runs on success and on every error
exit. The helper's cursor and diagnostic registers are deliberately shared and are
not restored from a recursive frame; doing that would undo input consumption or hide
the first failure.
