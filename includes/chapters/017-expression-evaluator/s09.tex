\section{Step 9 Reject zero before signed division}\label{expression-s09}

Test the right operand before issuing \texttt{ddiv}. A zero denominator transfers to the zero-status region, which will be completed shortly. A nonzero denominator produces a signed doubleword quotient and transfers to the shared range check. Using the wide quotient also makes the signed-word minimum divided by minus one representable long enough to diagnose its range.

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\subsection{Write or inspect}
Replace the temporary return in the \texttt{apply\_divide} region. Preserve its surrounding labels and frame scaffolding.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\MARSCodeLabel{MIPS assembly}

    nop
    ddiv $t0, $a0, $a1
    beq $zero, $zero, apply_check
    nop

\subsection{Expected observation}
With operands eight and three, \texttt{t0} contains two before the unfinished range region returns. The candidate is not yet the public result.

Why check the denominator first?

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A} To round negative quotients downward.
  \item \textbf{B} To accept an implicit zero result for a missing operand.
  \item \textbf{C} To report the specified error without executing a zero-divisor instruction.
\end{itemize}
