    \small
    \begin{enumerate}
      \setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
            \setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
      \item The blue \texttt{nop} at \texttt{vm\_ready} follows the function return. Descriptor outputs are published; the caller\textquotesingle s separate result stores are still ahead.
      \item \texttt{\$v0 = 1} reports halt; \texttt{\$v1 = 33} counts retired guest instructions, including \texttt{HALT}. Validation and native MIPS instructions do not contribute to that count. Fuel 64 is an upper bound, so halt can stop execution earlier.
      \item The eighteen-byte program starts at \texttt{0x10010004}, after its guard. Its final bytes \texttt{21 00} are \texttt{DROP} and \texttt{HALT}. The zero bytes beyond the slice are mapped padding. Program bytes and guards are unchanged.
      \item The first descriptor row contains PC 18, depth 1, retired 33 and status 1. The second contains capacity 8, fault $-1$ and the full stack pointer \texttt{0x100100d4}. PC 18 is just past the retired halt; it is not a failed fetch.
      \item The stack begins four bytes into the row at \texttt{0x100100d0}. Depth 1 makes only its first word, 6, live. Following zeros remain physical cells but are not additional operands. A drop reduces depth without clearing its old cell.
      \item Fuel precedes the caller\textquotesingle s two result words and guard. Both results still contain 99 (\texttt{63 00 00 00}); continuing copies \texttt{v0} and \texttt{v1} into them.
    \end{enumerate}
