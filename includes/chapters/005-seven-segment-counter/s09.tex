\section{Step 9 Write one byte to the
  port}\label{005-seven-segment-counter-s09-step-9-write-one-byte-to-the-port}

\texttt{sb} transfers the low byte of the glyph register to the fixed
device address. A word store would affect adjacent byte ports and
violate this interface, even when the right display happened to look
correct.

\subsection{Write or
  inspect}\label{005-seven-segment-counter-s09-write-or-inspect}

Append the store and its following checkpoint label.

\MARSCodeLabel{MIPS assembly}
\begin{minted}{mars-idea}
    sb $t1, 0($s0)
displayed:
\end{minted}

\subsection{Expected
  observation}\label{005-seven-segment-counter-s09-expected-observation}

The first program transaction is a byte write of 3f at 0xffff0010. At
\texttt{displayed}, index and remaining count have not yet been updated.

\subsection{Check your
  understanding}\label{005-seven-segment-counter-s09-check-your-understanding}

Why does transaction width matter?

\begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
        \textbf{A} The display reads only an instruction mnemonic, not memory.
  \item
        \textbf{B} Adjacent addresses control other parts of the peripheral.
  \item
        \textbf{C} A byte store is merely a faster spelling of every word
        store.
\end{itemize}
